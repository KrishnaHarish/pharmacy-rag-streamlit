# Pharmacy Information Database

## ASPIRIN

### General Information
Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to reduce pain, fever, and inflammation. It also has antiplatelet effects and is commonly used for cardiovascular protection.

### Dosage
- Pain/Fever: 325-650 mg every 4-6 hours as needed (maximum 4000 mg/day)
- Cardiovascular protection: 81-325 mg once daily
- Anti-inflammatory: 3-4 grams daily in divided doses

### Side Effects
Common side effects include:
- Stomach upset or heartburn
- Nausea
- Increased bleeding risk
- Bruising more easily

Serious side effects:
- Gastrointestinal bleeding
- Allergic reactions (rash, difficulty breathing)
- Reye's syndrome in children with viral infections
- Tinnitus (ringing in ears) with high doses

### Drug Interactions
Aspirin interacts with:
- Warfarin and other blood thinners (increased bleeding risk)
- Other NSAIDs (increased side effects)
- Methotrexate (increased toxicity)
- ACE inhibitors (reduced effectiveness)
- Alcohol (increased stomach bleeding risk)

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C or 68-77°F) away from moisture and light.

## IBUPROFEN

### General Information
Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to treat pain, fever, and inflammation. It works by reducing hormones that cause inflammation and pain in the body.

### Dosage
- Adults: 200-400 mg every 4-6 hours as needed (maximum 3200 mg/day for prescription strength)
- Children: Dosing based on weight (typically 5-10 mg/kg every 6-8 hours)

### Side Effects
Common side effects:
- Upset stomach
- Nausea
- Dizziness
- Headache
- Heartburn

Serious side effects:
- Cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke)
- Gastrointestinal bleeding
- Kidney problems
- Allergic reactions

### Drug Interactions
- Aspirin (reduced cardioprotective effect)
- Blood thinners (increased bleeding risk)
- ACE inhibitors (reduced effectiveness, increased kidney risk)
- Lithium (increased lithium levels)
- Diuretics (reduced effectiveness)

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) in a tightly closed container.

## INSULIN

### General Information
Insulin is a hormone used to control blood sugar levels in people with diabetes. There are several types including rapid-acting, short-acting, intermediate-acting, and long-acting insulin.

### Types and Onset
- Rapid-acting (lispro, aspart): Onset 15 minutes, peak 1 hour
- Short-acting (regular): Onset 30 minutes, peak 2-3 hours
- Intermediate-acting (NPH): Onset 2-4 hours, peak 4-12 hours
- Long-acting (glargine, detemir): Onset 2-4 hours, no pronounced peak

### Dosage
Dosing is highly individualized based on blood glucose levels, diet, and activity level. Must be prescribed and adjusted by healthcare provider.

### Side Effects
- Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) - most common
- Weight gain
- Injection site reactions
- Lipodystrophy (changes in fat tissue at injection sites)

### Storage
- Unopened insulin: Store in refrigerator (2-8°C or 36-46°F)
- Opened insulin: Can be kept at room temperature (below 30°C) for 28 days
- Never freeze insulin
- Protect from direct sunlight and heat
- Discard if exposed to extreme temperatures or appears cloudy (for clear insulins)

### Administration
- Rotate injection sites to prevent lipodystrophy
- Inject into subcutaneous tissue (abdomen, thighs, arms, buttocks)
- Do not inject into areas with scarring or lipodystrophy

## WARFARIN

### General Information
Warfarin is an anticoagulant (blood thinner) used to prevent blood clots. It works by inhibiting vitamin K-dependent clotting factors.

### Dosage
Highly individualized based on INR (International Normalized Ratio) monitoring. Typical range: 2-10 mg daily. Requires regular blood tests to adjust dosing.

### Side Effects
- Bleeding (most common and serious)
- Bruising
- Nausea
- Hair loss
- Purple toe syndrome (rare)

### Drug Interactions
Warfarin has extensive drug interactions:

Increases bleeding risk with:
- Aspirin and NSAIDs
- Antibiotics (many types)
- Antifungals
- Amiodarone
- SSRIs

Decreases warfarin effect:
- Vitamin K-rich foods (spinach, kale, broccoli)
- St. John's Wort
- Carbamazepine
- Rifampin

### Monitoring
- Regular INR monitoring required
- Target INR typically 2-3 for most indications
- Report any unusual bleeding immediately

### Dietary Considerations
- Maintain consistent vitamin K intake
- Avoid excessive alcohol
- Be cautious with cranberry juice and grapefruit juice

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) protected from light and moisture.

## METFORMIN

### General Information
Metformin is the first-line medication for type 2 diabetes. It works by decreasing glucose production in the liver and improving insulin sensitivity.

### Dosage
- Starting dose: 500 mg twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals
- Maximum dose: 2550 mg/day in divided doses
- Extended-release: 500-2000 mg once daily with evening meal

### Side Effects
Common:
- Diarrhea
- Nausea
- Upset stomach
- Metallic taste

Rare but serious:
- Lactic acidosis (medical emergency)
- Vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use

### Drug Interactions
- Contrast dye (increased lactic acidosis risk - hold before procedures)
- Alcohol (increased lactic acidosis risk)
- Cimetidine (increased metformin levels)

### Contraindications
- Severe kidney disease (eGFR <30 mL/min)
- Liver disease
- Excessive alcohol use
- Heart failure requiring medication
- Acidosis

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) in original container.

## LISINOPRIL

### General Information
Lisinopril is an ACE inhibitor used to treat high blood pressure and heart failure. It works by relaxing blood vessels to lower blood pressure.

### Dosage
- Hypertension: Initial 10 mg once daily, usual range 20-40 mg/day
- Heart failure: Initial 5 mg once daily, titrated up to 40 mg/day
- Can be taken with or without food

### Side Effects
Common:
- Dry cough (persistent)
- Dizziness
- Headache
- Fatigue

Serious:
- Angioedema (swelling of face, lips, tongue, throat)
- Hyperkalemia (high potassium)
- Kidney problems
- Hypotension (low blood pressure)

### Drug Interactions
- NSAIDs (reduced effectiveness, increased kidney risk)
- Potassium supplements (increased potassium levels)
- Diuretics (increased hypotension risk)
- Lithium (increased lithium levels)

### Contraindications
- Pregnancy (causes fetal harm)
- History of angioedema
- Bilateral renal artery stenosis

### Monitoring
- Blood pressure
- Kidney function (creatinine)
- Potassium levels

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) protected from moisture and excessive heat.

## LEVOTHYROXINE

### General Information
Levothyroxine is a synthetic thyroid hormone used to treat hypothyroidism. It replaces the hormone normally produced by the thyroid gland.

### Dosage
- Highly individualized based on TSH levels
- Typical range: 25-200 mcg once daily
- Take on empty stomach 30-60 minutes before breakfast
- Consistency in timing is important

### Side Effects
Usually occur when dose is too high:
- Anxiety or nervousness
- Insomnia
- Tremors
- Palpitations
- Weight loss
- Heat intolerance

### Drug Interactions
Decreased absorption with:
- Calcium supplements
- Iron supplements
- Antacids
- Soy products
- Coffee (take 4 hours apart)

Increased levothyroxine needs:
- Estrogen therapy
- Pregnancy
- Certain seizure medications

### Monitoring
- TSH levels checked regularly (every 6-8 weeks when adjusting, then annually)
- Dosage adjusted based on TSH results

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) protected from light and moisture. Do not use if expired.

## ATORVASTATIN

### General Information
Atorvastatin is a statin medication used to lower cholesterol and reduce cardiovascular risk. It works by inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, reducing cholesterol production in the liver.

### Dosage
- Initial: 10-20 mg once daily
- Range: 10-80 mg once daily
- Can be taken any time of day, with or without food
- Best if taken at same time each day

### Side Effects
Common:
- Muscle aches (myalgia)
- Headache
- Nausea
- Diarrhea

Serious:
- Rhabdomyolysis (severe muscle breakdown) - rare
- Liver damage - rare
- Diabetes (slightly increased risk)

### Drug Interactions
- Grapefruit juice (increases atorvastatin levels - avoid large amounts)
- Gemfibrozil (increased muscle toxicity risk)
- Certain antibiotics and antifungals
- Cyclosporine

### Monitoring
- Liver function tests at baseline and as needed
- Lipid panel to assess effectiveness
- Report muscle pain or weakness immediately

### Lifestyle
- Continue healthy diet and exercise
- Avoid excessive alcohol

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) in original container.

## OMEPRAZOLE

### General Information
Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) used to treat acid reflux, GERD, and ulcers. It works by reducing stomach acid production.

### Dosage
- GERD: 20 mg once daily for 4-8 weeks
- Ulcer healing: 20-40 mg once daily
- Take before meals (30-60 minutes before eating)
- Do not crush or chew delayed-release capsules

### Side Effects
Common:
- Headache
- Nausea
- Diarrhea or constipation
- Abdominal pain

Long-term use concerns:
- Vitamin B12 deficiency
- Magnesium deficiency
- Increased bone fracture risk
- Increased infection risk (C. difficile)

### Drug Interactions
- Clopidogrel (reduced effectiveness - avoid combination)
- Warfarin (may increase bleeding risk)
- Methotrexate (increased toxicity)
- Reduces absorption of: iron, calcium, vitamin B12

### Usage Guidelines
- Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration
- Not for immediate heartburn relief (takes 1-4 days to work)
- May take up to 4 weeks for full effect

### Storage
Store at room temperature (20-25°C) in tightly closed container, protected from moisture.
